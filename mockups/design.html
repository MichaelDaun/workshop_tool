Design manual

Verkstan

Workshops for teams of 2–4. Each one is a problem the team solves on screen and then on a physical box. This page holds the rules and the shared parts. The UI is Swedish, everything else is English.

Principles

  1. The scene is the workshop. Values sit in the picture, next to the part they describe.
  2. Show it rather than say it. If a glow, a bar or a colour carries the meaning, it needs no label, legend or caption.
  3. Colour is signal. The domain colour marks what is active. Everything else is graphite and paper.
  4. The reward happens in the scene. The lamp lights, the robot reaches the star, the lid opens.
  5. Few words. No hints, no sub-lines, no leads, no commentary. Assume capable adults and teenagers.
  6. One type family. Atkinson Hyperlegible for text, its mono cut for values.

Workshops and tasks

A workshop is one problem with three tasks. The first two are solved on screen and need the team to talk: a prediction, a limit or information only one person has. The last one makes the box do something concrete.

ProblemA heading that states the goal. ”Exakt en ampere”
Task 1The core idea on screen. ”Få exakt 1 A genom lampan.”
Task 2The same idea under a constraint or with a prediction first. ”Med 4 batterier: räkna ut motståndet för 1 A innan ni slår på brytaren.”
Task 3The box. ”Lysdioden på lådan tål 20 mA. Få den att lysa så starkt det går.”
Vad hände?Two or three sentences and at most one formula, folded away.

  1. Verb first in a task. One sentence, two at most.
  2. Write ”workshop” in Swedish too.
  3. No exclamation marks. Machines do not speak in the first person.
  4. Swedish typography: ”citat”, decimal comma, a space before the unit (3,0 V), an en dash in ranges (10–15 min).
  5. Avoid: magisk, resa, utforska, upptäck, dyk ner, låt oss, kraftfull, sömlös, superenkelt.

The box

Every workshop has a box: a Pico W with a few parts, named by its node id. The panel shows a top-down drawing. In the mockup the drawn parts can be pressed.
